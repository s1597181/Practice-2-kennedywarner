Art into Industry   The 1923 slogan

One school, three cities, fourteen years

The Bauhaus was founded in 1919 in weimar by the German archetect Walter Gropius. Its aim, in the Met's words, was a radical one: to reimagine the material world to reflect the unity of the arts. Groupius set it out in the Proclamation of the Bauhaus that same year, describing a craft guild that would fold architecture, sculpture, and painting back into a single thing.

Every student started int he same place. The preliminary course out them through materials, color theory, and formal realtionships before they were allowed to specialise, and it was taught by painters, Paul Klee and Vasily Kandinsky and Josef Albers among them. Students arrived from wildly different backgrounds, and the course was the leveller.

The original craft-first plan turned out to be finacially impossible. In 1923 Gropius repositioned the school around designing for mass production, and it took a new slogan: Art intoIndustry. In 1925 the whole school moved to Dessau, into a building gropius designed for it.

What came out of the workshop

Furniture

Marcel Breuer ran the cabinetmaking workshop from 1924 to 1928 and spent it trying to reduce a chair to almost nothing. He thought chairs would eventually be replaced by columns of air. Looking at the steel tubes on his bicycle, he started bending metal instead of carving wood, and ended up with light chairs a cafotry could actually produce.

Weaving

Gunta Stölzl ran the textile workshop and pushed students into cellophane, fibreglass and metal thread. The fabrics sold, and the money kept the school going. The workshop was almost entirely women, partly because women were discouraged from the other studios. Anni Albers came out of it and wrote about modernist textiles for the rest of her life.

Metal

Marianne Brandt was the first woman admitted to the metal workshop and took it over from László Moholy-Nagy in 1928. Her silver and ebony teapot was never mass produced, but it is the shape most people picture when they picture the Bauhaus: a nondrip spout, a handle that does not conduct heat, and not one decorative move anywhere on it.

Typography

Typography was not a priority at first. Under Moholy-Nagy and Herbert Bayer it became one. The rule was visual clarity above everything, and the school's own posters, sans serif with photographs used as graphic elements rather than illustration, became the way the Bauhaus looked from the outside.

How it ended

Gropius stepped down in 1928. Hannes Meyer took over and pushed the school toward the public good rather than private luxury, then resigned in 1930 under pressure from an increasingly right-wing local government. Ludwig Mies van der Rohe replaced him, moved what was left to Berlin, and closed it in 1933.

The people scattered, mostly to the United States, and took the teaching with them. Breuer and Gropius went to Harvard. Josef and Anni Albers went to Black Mountain College, and Josef later to Yale. Moholy-Nagy started the New Bauhaus in Chicago in 1937. Mies designed the campus at the Illinois Institute of Technology and taught there.

That is why a school that lasted fourteen years is still worth your afternoon. The preliminary course is the reason your first design class was about materials and color before it was about clients. The idea that a useful object should also be a good one, and that both are one job rather than two, came through those workshops.
