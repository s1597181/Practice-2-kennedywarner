Art into Industry   The 1923 slogan

One school, three cities, fourteen years

The Bauhaus was founded in 1919 in weimar by the German archetect Walter Gropius. Its aim, in the Met's words, was a radical one: to reimagine the material world to reflect the unity of the arts. Groupius set it out in the Proclamation of the Bauhaus that same year, describing a craft guild that would fold architecture, sculpture, and painting back into a single thing.

Every student started int he same place. The preliminary course out them through materials, color theory, and formal realtionships before they were allowed to specialise, and it was taught by painters, Paul Klee and Vasily Kandinsky and Josef Albers among them. Students arrived from wildly different backgrounds, and the course was the leveller.

The original craft-first plan turned out to be finacially impossible. In 1923 Gropius repositioned the school around designing for mass production, and it took a new slogan: Art intoIndustry. In 1925 the whole school moved to Dessau, into a building gropius designed for it.

What came out of the workshop

Furniture

Marcel Breuer ran the cabinetmaking workshop from 1924 to 1928 and spent it trying to reduce a chair to almost nothing. He thought chairs would eventually be replaced by columns of air. Looking at the steel tubes on his bicycle, he started bending metal instead of carving wood, and ended up with light chairs a cafotry could actually produce.
